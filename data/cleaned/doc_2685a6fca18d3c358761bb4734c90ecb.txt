California is a hub for education. With more than 2.1 million college students, the state leads the nation with the highest number of college-going students. Yet, not all paths to a postsecondary education are linear. Every student has a unique educational journey. The Student Pathways Data Story provides information on key milestones for students as they navigate from high school to college and into their careers over time.

HOW HAVE CALIFORNIA STUDENTS NAVIGATED TO AND THROUGH COLLEGE?

Explore the different educational paths Californians take after graduating from high school.
The visualization below shows the paths of students who graduated from California public high schools in the 2014-2015 academic year. The diagram highlights the different paths these students took, whether they went into the workforce after high school, enrolled in a 2-year public college, or attended a 4-year public college. The outcomes for all paths differ to create a unique story for each California student.

• Students who graduated from a California public high school in 2014-2015 with a standard diploma. Does not include GED or other types of high school completion.
• Students who graduated from a California public high school in 2014-2015 and then enrolled in a California public 2-year or 4-year college within nine years of graduating from high school.
• Students who graduated from a California public high school in 2014-2015 and then earned an Associate degree, Bachelor’s degree, or Community College Certificate within nine years of graduating from high school. Future updates to the data story will include data on advanced degrees that students may earn in this time period.
• Note: “Never Enrolled in a California Public College” currently includes students who attended private colleges within California and out-of-state colleges, as well as those who choose not to go to college. Future updates to the data story will include data on attendance in private or out-of-state colleges.

STUDENT PATHWAYS FROM HIGH SCHOOL GRADUATION TO A COLLEGE DEGREE

This diagram shows different educational paths taken by 2014–2015 California public high school graduates in the nine years after they graduated from high school. You can hover over any part of the diagram to see counts for each outcome.

Read the diagram from left to right. On the far left, the diagram shows the full group of students in the high school graduating class of 2014-2015. The next section to the right shows one of three paths to college enrollment. The top branch highlights the path of students who never enroll in a California public college within nine years. This group may enroll in private or out-of-state colleges, or they may decide not to enroll in college at all. In future versions of this data story, C2C plans to include data that will allow you to investigate these distinctions. The middle and bottom paths indicate whether students’ first college enrollment was in a 2-year California public college (a community college) or a 4-year California public college (a UC or CSU campus). Finally, the last section on the right shows four different certificate and degree completion outcomes: how many completed a community college certificate, how many completed an Associate degree, how many completed a Bachelor’s degree, and how many attended but did not yet complete a degree at a California public college.

You may notice significant differences between the California statewide statistics and a specific district’s statistics. This may be because the underlying demographic characteristics of the district are different than those of the entire state. For example, a district where a smaller proportion of college-going students are Asian may have a smaller proportion of Asian students than California overall.

MEDIAN TIME TO DEGREE FOR GRADUATES OF CA PUBLIC COLLEGES

This data visualization shows how long it takes students to complete a degree or certificate at a California public college. We calculate the time to award from when a student first enrolls in the institution where they earn their award. For students who attend multiple institutions or transfer, this means that the full length of their degree journey is not included in the current version of this chart. Future dashboards will explore the transfer journey in more detail.

The height of each bar shows the numbers of students who completed their award in the specific number of years. The chart also includes an indicator of the median time it took students in the visualization to earn their award. The median is the number that divides the group in half, so that half of students took the median or more time to complete their award, and half of students took less time than the median to complete their award.

PERCENT OF STUDENTS WHO WORK WHILE ENROLLED IN COLLEGE

This infographic shows data for students who work while enrolled in college. You can choose between seeing the percentage of students who work and their median annual wages. Annual wages are calculated by adding up students’ quarterly earnings. The median is the number that divides the group in half, so that half of students earn the median wage or more money every year, and half of students earn less than the median wage.

The percent of students working are calculated as a proportion of all of the students enrolled in a specific type of institution (2-year California public college or 4-year California public college) in a specific academic year. “Enrolled students” is limited to students who graduated from a California public high school after 2014.

For the Student Pathways Dashboard, students who work while enrolled in college are defined as students who report earnings on a W-2 for at least 3 of the 4 quarters of the year.

MEDIAN WAGES FOR CA PUBLIC COLLEGE GRADUATES EMPLOYED IN CA

This line graph shows median annual earnings for college graduates from California public colleges in each of the years following the year they earned their degree or certificate. The visualization only includes data for those who work year-round. It does not include those who work seasonally or have gaps in their employment. Different types of degrees and certificates appear as different colored lines. If someone earned a degree less than 5 years ago – for example, in the 2021-2022 academic year – the visualization only shows data for the years after graduation.

The data only includes people with earnings on a W-2 reported for all four quarters of the year. For these students, we calculate annual wages by adding up their quarterly earnings and reporting the median wage. The median is the number that divides the group in half, so that half of students earn the median wage or more money every year, and half of students earn less than the median wage.
