UNDOCUMENTED STUDENTS AND FINANCIAL AID

ON THIS PAGE

ON THIS PAGE

Undocumented students, including Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals (DACA) recipients, do not meet
eligibility requirements for federal student aid
. However, you may be eligible for other types of student financial aid (state aid, college or career/trade school financial aid, or private scholarships).

APPLYING FOR STATE OR SCHOOL AID

Most states and colleges or career/trade schools use information collected on the
Free Application for Free Federal Student Aid
(FAFSA
®
) form
to determine whether you’re eligible for aid. However, we first recommend that you check with your high school’s college counselor or your college’s or career school’s financial aid office to see what types of financial aid you may be eligible to receive and whether completing the FAFSA form is required to apply for that aid.

NEEDING AN SSN TO SUBMIT A FAFSA
®
FORM

If you are completing an online FAFSA form, a Social Security number (SSN) is required to create a StudentAid.gov account to access your FAFSA form and to electronically sign it.

Most undocumented students aren’t eligible for an SSN; thus, they cannot complete the FAFSA form. However, DACA recipients with SSNs can complete the FAFSA form. For information about obtaining an SSN, visit
ssa.gov/pubs/EN-05-10009.pdf
.

If your FAFSA form is rejected due to an issue with your SSN, contact the Social Security Administration at 1-800-772-1213 (TTY for the deaf or hard of hearing 1-800-325-0778).

Your parent or spouse doesn’t need an SSN to create a StudentAid.gov account and complete their section of your FAFSA form.

If the parent or spouse is completing their own section on the FAFSA form and doesn't have an SSN, the SSN will be blank and disabled. If the parent or spouse has an Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN), they should add it to the FAFSA form in the ITIN field only. If they don’t have an ITIN, they should leave that question blank.

Note for the 2025–26 FAFSA form only: If you’re inviting your parent or spouse to your FAFSA form and they don’t have an SSN, select the box that indicates they don’t not have an SSN. Don’t enter an ITIN in the SSN field.

Note: The rest of this page focuses on completing the FAFSA
®
form, so the guidance applies only to DACA recipients with SSNs—not to all undocumented students.

FILLING OUT THE FAFSA
®
FORM

ANSWERING THE CITIZENSHIP STATUS QUESTION

As an undocumented student, you must select the “Neither U.S. citizen nor eligible noncitizen” option on the FAFSA form.

Your parent’s or spouse’s citizenship status doesn’t affect your ability to complete the FAFSA form. In fact, the FAFSA form doesn’t even ask about your parent’s or spouse’s citizenship status.

ANSWERING THE STATE OF RESIDENCE QUESTION

The state of residence is the state where your permanent address is located. The fact that you are an undocumented student doesn’t affect how you and your parent should answer this question for purposes of completing the FAFSA form.

If your school or state offers aid based on your state of residence, they may have additional residency requirements. You should contact your high school’s college counselor or the financial aid office at the college or career school you plan to attend for assistance with state of residence qualifications.

SUBMITTING TAX INFORMATION

If you’re completing the FAFSA form online, you and (if applicable) your parent(s) or spouse must provide consent and approval to transfer your federal tax information from the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) directly into the FAFSA form even if any of the following applies:

• Your parent or spouse doesn’t have an SSN or ITIN.
Your parent or spouse doesn’t have an SSN or ITIN.

• You, your parent, and/or spouse didn’t file taxes.
You, your parent, and/or spouse didn’t file taxes.

• You, your parent, and/or spouse filed taxes outside of the U.S.
You, your parent, and/or spouse filed taxes outside of the U.S.

If you’re completing the FAFSA form on paper, follow the instructions that detail how to answer the financial information questions.
